# HEC Bot

Welcome to HEC Bot, your slightly nerdy study companion for **History of Economic Thought**.

It has read through the course materials so you do not have to stare at twelve lecture files at 2 a.m. wondering whether mercantilism is a person, a country, or a particularly aggressive economic opinion.

## What HEC Bot Can Do

HEC Bot can help you find, search, and understand the course materials:

- Summarize lecture materials
- Find the lecture or section where a topic is discussed
- Search for economists, theories, schools of thought, and key terms
- Explain difficult economic concepts
- Answer questions about economists and theories
- Compare different schools of economic thought
- Connect theories to historical events
- Help you prepare for lectures and exams
- Turn academic confusion into slightly more organized confusion

## Example Questions

Try asking:

- What is the History of Economic Thought about?
- Why did economists keep disagreeing with each other?
- What is the difference between classical and Keynesian economics?
- Which economist said this, and were they right?
- In which lecture is mercantilism discussed?
- Find every lecture that mentions Keynes.
- Summarize Lecture 5 before I pretend I have already read it.
- Explain this concept as if I have an exam tomorrow.
- What would different schools of thought say about a current economic problem?

## How It Works

Ask a question in plain English, and HEC Bot searches through the lecture materials before giving you a clear, study-friendly answer. It can search for a specific word, find relevant lectures, or explain the results instead of making you hunt through every file yourself.

### Example 1: Summarizing a Lecture

**You ask:**

> Summarize Lecture 1 in five bullet points.

**HEC Bot answers:**

> Lecture 1 introduces the goals of the course, explains why economic thought changes over time, presents the idea that no single economic theory is universally accepted, and describes how the course connects economic theories with real historical events.

### Example 2: Explaining a Concept

**You ask:**

> Why is the history of economic thought important?

**HEC Bot answers:**

> It helps you understand how economic theories developed, connect economic ideas to different historical systems, compare competing schools of thought, and evaluate economic opinions more carefully. It also helps explain why economists have been disagreeing with impressive consistency for centuries.

### Example 3: Finding Information

**You ask:**

> Find the lectures that mention Keynes.

**HEC Bot answers:**

> I found references to Keynes in Lectures 7, 8, and 10. Lecture 8 gives the most detailed discussion of Keynesian economics and its role in the development of macroeconomic thought.

## Course Materials

The lecture materials are stored in the [`spec`](spec/) directory and include Lectures 1 through 12.

They cover:

- Major economic theories
- Important economists and thinkers
- Schools of economic thought
- Historical economic systems
- The relationship between economic ideas and real-world events
- The timeless academic tradition of disagreeing confidently

## Knowledge Sources

The course materials refer to works by authors including:

- H. Landreth and D. C. Colander
- A. Roncaglia
- R. Heilbroner
- M. Blaug

In other words, several very clever people who spent a lot of time explaining why the economy does not behave as expected.

## Educational Disclaimer

HEC Bot is a study assistant, not an all-knowing economic oracle.

Always check important answers against the original lecture materials and recommended textbooks. HEC Bot may be helpful, but it cannot attend the exam for you.
